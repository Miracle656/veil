# Veil Connect

Veil Connect lets your web app use a person's Veil wallet. There is no
extension to install. Your app opens a Veil window; the person creates or
unlocks their wallet there with a fingerprint, face or screen lock; your app
gets an answer back.

<Callout type="info">
Veil Connect is in **pilot**. Apps are added one at a time, amounts are
limited, and the interface may still change. To be added, see
[Getting your app registered](#getting-your-app-registered).
</Callout>

## What you can do

| Call | The person sees | You get |
|---|---|---|
| `connect()` | "Share your wallet address with *your app*?" | `{ address, network }` |
| `pay({...})` | Who is asking, how much, to which address, then Veil's Send screen | `{ hash, reference, network, from, memo }` |
| `invoke({...})` | Which contract and function, the values, and any money it takes from them | `{ hash, network, contract, method, signer, from, result }` |
| `addMoney()` | "Buy with naira" in Veil | `{ type: 'fund' }` once they are on their way |
| `cashOut()` | "Cash out" in Veil: their USDC to their own bank, as naira | `{ type: 'cashout' }` once they are on their way |

Each person has their own wallet. Veil never holds your money, and you never
hold a key to anyone's wallet. Every payment and every contract call is
approved by the person with their passkey.

## Quick start

```html
<script src="https://app.useveilapp.xyz/connect.js"></script>
<script>
  const veil = VeilConnect.init({ app: 'your-app-id' })

  connectButton.onclick = async () => {
    const { address } = await veil.connect()
    // address is the person's wallet: a C… contract address
  }
</script>
```

Three things to know before you write more:

- **Call from a click or tap.** Browsers block a window opened any other way.
- **A call that does not complete rejects** with an error whose `code` is
  `cancelled` (the person closed the window or pressed Cancel), `refused`
  (Veil would not take the request; read `message`), or `blocked` (the browser
  blocked the window).
- **A result is a claim from the browser.** Your server must check it on the
  network before acting. See [Verify on your server](#verify-on-your-server).

## Two addresses, and why it matters

A Veil wallet has two addresses, and mixing them up is the most common
integration mistake.

| | Starts with | What it is | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wallet** | `C…` | The person's wallet. Controlled by their passkey. Never changes. | `address` from `connect()`, `signer` from `invoke()` |
| **Spending account** | `G…` | Carries transactions and pays network fees. | `from` in `pay()` and `invoke()` results |

- Identify a person by their **wallet** address.
- A `pay()` payment **leaves from the spending account**. Do not expect the
  sender to be the address `connect()` gave you.
- Pay a person out to their **wallet** address. See
  [Paying a person](#paying-a-person).

## Taking a payment

```js
const { hash, reference, from, memo } = await veil.pay({
  to: depositAddress,     // your full Stellar address, G… or C…
  amount: '20',           // a string, at most 7 decimals
  asset: 'USDC',          // 'USDC' (default) or 'XLM'
  reference: 'order-123', // 1–64 letters, digits, "-" or "_"
})
// Send hash + reference to YOUR server and verify there.
```

- One payment may be at most **1000** (USDC or XLM) during the pilot.
- Your `reference` is written on the payment as a text memo when it is **28
  characters or fewer**. A longer one is not cut to fit: no memo is written,
  and `memo` in the result is `null`. Keep references short, and keep personal
  details out of them, because a memo is public.
- The person may pay a small Veil fee **on top of** your amount. You always
  receive the full amount you asked for.

## Calling your own contract

`invoke()` asks the person to approve one call to one of your contracts. Use
it for anything that is more than a plain payment: joining a challenge,
staking, claiming a reward.

```js
const { address } = await veil.connect()

const { hash, result } = await veil.invoke({
  contract: 'C…',               // your contract, registered for your app id
  method: 'join_challenge',
  args: [
    { type: 'address', value: address },
    { type: 'u32', value: 7 },
    { type: 'i128', value: '50000000' },   // 5 USDC: amounts have 7 decimals
  ],
})
```

You pass a contract, a function name and typed values, and Veil builds the
transaction. Veil does not sign a ready-made transaction handed to it by an
app.

### Argument types

| Type | Value | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `address` | A full `G…` or `C…` address | `{ type: 'address', value: address }` |
| `bool` | `true` or `false` | `{ type: 'bool', value: true }` |
| `u32`, `i32` | A whole number | `{ type: 'u32', value: 7 }` |
| `u64`, `i64`, `u128`, `i128` | A whole number, **as a string** | `{ type: 'i128', value: '50000000' }` |
| `string` | Up to 256 characters | `{ type: 'string', value: 'morning run' }` |
| `symbol` | Up to 32 letters, digits or `_` | `{ type: 'symbol', value: 'gold' }` |
| `bytes` | Hex, no `0x` | `{ type: 'bytes', value: 'deadbeef' }` |

At most 12 arguments. Lists, maps and structs are not carried yet. If your
function needs one, tell us.

### Writing your contract for Veil

Ask for approval from the address `connect()` gave you. It is a contract
address, and `require_auth()` on it is answered by the person's passkey.

```rust
pub fn join_challenge(env: Env, user: Address, challenge: u32, stake: i128) {
    user.require_auth();

    // Take the stake from the user. They approve this together with
    // join_challenge, in one prompt. `usdc` is the token contract's address,
    // which your contract stores when it is set up.
    token::Client::new(&env, &usdc).transfer(&user, &env.current_contract_address(), &stake);
    // ...
}
```

- Take tokens with the token's `transfer`, as above. **Do not use `approve`
  and `transfer_from`**: Veil refuses any call that gives a contract a
  standing allowance.
- Keep the amount within the pilot limit: 1000 USDC or 1000 XLM per request.
- The person needs a little XLM in their spending account for the network
  fee. If they have none, Veil tells them to add some. Veil adds no fee of its
  own to a contract call.

### What the person sees

Before they are asked for their passkey, Veil works out what your call would
really do and shows it:

- your app's name, the contract and the function;
- every value you sent;
- every token amount leaving their wallet, and the full address it goes to;
- the network fee;
- on request, the full list of calls they are approving.

### What Veil always refuses

Whatever your contract does, Veil will not ask the person to approve:

- a contract that is not registered for your app on that network;
- `approve` on any token;
- any call into the person's own wallet contract;
- deploying a contract;
- more than 1000 XLM or 1000 USDC leaving in one request;
- a call that needs approval from an address that is not the person's.

If what the call would do changes between the screen the person read and the
moment they approve, nothing is signed and they are asked to start again.

### The result

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `hash` | The transaction hash. Verify it on your server. |
| `signer` | The wallet (`C…`) that approved. The same address `connect()` gives. |
| `from` | The spending account (`G…`) that carried the transaction. |
| `result` | What your function returned, as base64 XDR of an `ScVal`, or `null`. |
| `contract`, `method`, `network` | What was called, and where. |

Decode `result` with the Stellar SDK:

```js
import { xdr, scValToNative } from '@stellar/stellar-sdk'

const value = result ? scValToNative(xdr.ScVal.fromXDR(result, 'base64')) : null
```

## Adding money and cashing out

```js
await veil.addMoney()   // opens "Buy with naira" in Veil
await veil.cashOut()    // opens "Cash out" in Veil
```

Both hand the person over to Veil and resolve once they are on their way. You
do not pass an amount or bank details, and you do not see them. The person
chooses both inside Veil.

Adding naira is available to people in Nigeria and includes Veil's own
identity check the first time.

## Verify on your server

<Callout type="warning">
Everything your page receives comes from the person's browser. Treat it as a
claim, not a fact. Never credit a payment, start a subscription or record a
contract call on the browser's word alone.
</Callout>

### A payment

<Steps>

### Look the hash up

For example `GET https://horizon.stellar.org/transactions/<hash>/operations`.
Check the transaction succeeded.

### Check where the money went

It must be the **right asset**, the **right amount**, **to your address**. For
USDC that means the code `USDC` **and** the issuer
`GA5ZSEJYB37JRC5AVCIA5MOP4RHTM335X2KGX3IHOJAPP5RE34K4KZVN`.

- If your address is a `G…` account, look for a `payment` operation and check
  its `to`, `asset_code`, `asset_issuer` and `amount`.
- If your address is a `C…` contract, look for an `invoke_host_function`
  operation and check its `asset_balance_changes` list.

Match by **destination**, not by sender. The same transaction can hold a
second, smaller payment to another address: that is Veil's fee. Credit the
payment to your address and ignore every other operation.

### Check the memo

It must be a text memo equal to your reference. That is what ties a hash to
one order.

### Accept each hash once

Store it with the reference and refuse a repeat. Check `network` is `mainnet`.

</Steps>

### A contract call

Look the hash up, check the transaction succeeded, and check it called
**your contract** and **your function**. Then read what happened from your
contract's own state or events, not from `result`. Accept each hash once.

## Paying a person

To send someone money, for example a withdrawal from your app, pay the
**wallet** address `connect()` gave you.

- Use the USDC token contract's `transfer(from, to, amount)`. On mainnet that
  contract is `CCW67TSZV3SSS2HXMBQ5JFGCKJNXKZM7UQUWUZPUTHXSTZLEO7SJMI75`. A
  classic `payment` operation cannot pay a `C…` address.
- Amounts have 7 decimal places: send `amount × 10^7`.
- The transfer lands as soon as your transaction succeeds. The wallet needs
  no trustline and no XLM to receive, and Veil charges nothing to receive.
- The address never changes, and it cannot be moved to another person.
- One person can have more than one wallet. Pay the address they linked, and
  let them link again with `connect()` if they change wallets.

## Getting your app registered

Veil accepts requests only from apps it knows, and only from each app's own
web address. That is what stops someone asking for a payment in your app's
name from a link in a chat.

Send us:

1. **Who you are** and what your app does.
2. **Your web addresses**, exactly as they appear in the browser, for example
   `https://yourapp.com` and `https://www.yourapp.com`.
3. **For testing**, the local address you develop on, for example
   `http://localhost:3000`. You get a separate test app id for it, shown to
   people as "*Your app* (test)".
4. **If you call contracts**, the contract id on each network and its source.
   We read a contract before registering it, because your users will be asked
   to approve what it does.

You get back an app id to pass to `VeilConnect.init`.

<Callout type="warning">
A test app id talks to the **live** wallet. Payments made with it are real.
Use small amounts.
</Callout>

## Not available yet

- Signing a ready-made transaction.
- Contract calls whose arguments are lists, maps or structs.
- More than one contract call in a single approval.
- Paying without the passkey each time.
- A webhook to your server. Verify by hash.
- Native mobile apps. This is for web apps in a browser.
