# Veil Names

A Veil Name replaces a contract address with something a person can type:

```
jack*useveilapp.xyz
```

instead of

```
CA7BWBV2L67YVIZWJKLYT3RRCTFCZPN6L5SXYQ4YELWP56E34JFNOEP4
```

It resolves over [SEP-2 federation](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/ecosystem/sep-0002.md), the Stellar standard for named addresses, so any wallet that speaks it can resolve a Veil Name without knowing anything about Veil.

<Callout type="warning">
A Veil Name points at a **smart contract address** (`C…`). A classic Stellar
payment cannot address a contract, so **you cannot withdraw from an exchange to
a Veil Name.** It works from Veil, and from anything that speaks Soroban. This
is a property of how Stellar's classic and Soroban layers differ, not a gap we
expect to close — see [The exchange limitation](#the-exchange-limitation).
</Callout>

## What it costs

Names are **rented by the year**, not bought outright. The price depends on length:

| Length | Per year |
| --- | --- |
| 3–5 characters | **3 USDC** |
| 6–10 characters | **5 USDC** |
| 11–20 characters | **2 USDC** |

The middle band is the dearest, which looks like a mistake and is not. Most real
first names are six to ten characters, so that is where demand sits. The table
prices **demand, not scarcity** — short names are not automatically the most
valuable, they are simply the ones most often assumed to be.

A term is **one to five years**, paid in full at registration. The total is
always `price per year × years`, computed on-chain:

```
ada      3 characters  × 5 years  =  15 USDC
adebayo  7 characters  × 1 year   =   5 USDC
adebayoakinola  14 characters × 2 years = 4 USDC
```

<Callout type="info">
Payment is in **USDC** on Stellar mainnet — the contract cannot take XLM for a
name. You need a USDC balance, but **not** a trustline: a Veil wallet is a
contract account, and the Stellar Asset Contract holds balances for contracts
in its own storage. Trustlines are a classic-account requirement.
</Callout>

## Buying a name

<Steps>

### Pick a name

It must be 3–20 characters of lowercase `a–z`, digits `0–9`, and underscore.
No underscore at the start or end, and never two in a row. See
[What a name may be](#what-a-name-may-be) for why the rules are this narrow.

### Choose a term

One to five years. Longer terms are not discounted — five years costs exactly
five times one year. The reason to take a longer term is to stop thinking about
renewals, not to save money.

### Pay and register

The contract takes the full amount in USDC and sends it to the Veil treasury in
the same transaction that creates the name. There is no separate invoice, no
pending state, and no refund path: the registration either completes or the
payment never leaves your wallet.

### Receive the name as an NFT

Registration mints a token to you. That token **is** the name — hold it and the
name resolves to you, transfer it and the name follows it to the new holder.
You do not need to do anything else to point the name at your wallet.

</Steps>

## Renewal, and what "subscription" means here

A Veil Name is closer to a domain name than to a subscription. **Nothing charges
you automatically.** There is no card on file, no recurring authorisation, and
no way for the contract to take money from you after registration. If you want
the name to continue, someone has to pay to renew it.

That cuts both ways: you can never be charged by surprise, and you can never be
protected by autopay. The [recovery window](#what-happens-when-a-name-expires)
exists because of the second half.

**Anyone can renew anyone's name.** Renewal takes a payer, a name and a term —
the payer does not have to be the holder. Renewing a friend's name is a gift
they do not have to accept or even notice.

How the new expiry is calculated depends on when you renew:

| When you renew | New expiry is measured from |
| --- | --- |
| While the name is still active | its **existing expiry** — you lose no time by renewing early |
| During the 90-day recovery window | **today** — the lapsed time is gone |
| After recovery has closed | you cannot renew; the name is open to anyone |

A renewal may not push the total term past **five years** in one go. A name with
four years left can be extended by one, not by three.

<Callout type="info">
A "year" here is a flat **365 days**, never a calendar year. The contract, the
server and the app must all compute one expiry, and "a year from 29 February"
has no single answer.
</Callout>

## What happens when a name expires

A name moves through three states, and the middle one is the whole design.

| State | Resolves? | Who may register it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Active** | yes | nobody |
| **Recovery** — 90 days after expiry | **no** | the previous holder only |
| **Open** | no | anyone |

The instant a name expires it **stops resolving**. It does not keep working
until someone else buys it.

That matters more than it first appears. If a lapsed name went straight back on
sale, it would keep working in every contact list that already had it — and
would quietly begin paying whoever bought it next. The person who lost the name
would not notice, and neither would anyone sending to it.

So the 90-day window does two jobs at once:

- Someone who simply forgot a 3 USDC renewal gets their name back, rather than
  losing it to whoever was watching the expiry list.
- Every stale contact fails for a full quarter before a repoint is even
  possible, so a sender finds out by a failed payment rather than by a
  successful one to a stranger.

## What a name may be

The character rules are deliberately narrow, and the reason is always the same:
a name that reads as somebody else's name is a payment redirect.

- **3 to 20 characters.** Shorter names are reserved rather than merely invalid.
- **Lowercase `a–z`, digits `0–9`, and underscore only.**
- **No leading, trailing, or doubled underscore** — `_jack`, `jack_` and
  `ja__ck` are all refused.
- **No Unicode.** A Cyrillic `а` is rejected outright, not converted to a Latin
  `a`. Folding lookalikes would be the bug: it would let one name resolve to a
  different holder's payment destination.
- **Case is not part of a name.** `Jack`, `JACK` and `jack` are the same name.

Around forty names — `veil`, `admin`, `support`, `security`, `payments`,
`verify` and similar — are **permanently reserved** and can never be registered,
so nobody can pose as Veil or its team.

<Callout type="info">
These rules are enforced **on-chain**, not just in the app. The page and the API
check them too, but only so they can fail fast — anyone can call the contract
directly, so the contract is the authority.
</Callout>

## How resolution works

Veil publishes a federation server in its
[`stellar.toml`](https://www.useveilapp.xyz/.well-known/stellar.toml):

```toml
FEDERATION_SERVER = "https://app.useveilapp.xyz/api/federation"
```

A wallet resolving `jack*useveilapp.xyz` asks:

```
GET https://app.useveilapp.xyz/api/federation?q=jack*useveilapp.xyz&type=name
```

and receives:

```json
{
  "stellar_address": "jack*useveilapp.xyz",
  "account_id": "CA7BWBV2L67YVIZWJKLYT3RRCTFCZPN6L5SXYQ4YELWP56E34JFNOEP4"
}
```

The endpoint reads the registry contract directly, so there is no database to
fall out of step with the ledger. An expired name returns a 404, because the
contract itself stops answering for it.

Reverse lookup (`type=id`, address → name) returns 404 by design: the registry
is keyed name → address with no reverse index, so answering would mean scanning
every name.

## The exchange limitation

A Veil wallet is a Soroban contract account, so a Veil Name resolves to a `C…`
address. Stellar's classic `payment` operation cannot address a contract at all.

In practice:

| Sending from | Works? |
| --- | --- |
| Veil (mobile or web) | yes |
| Any Soroban-aware wallet or contract | yes |
| An exchange withdrawal | **no** |
| A classic-only wallet | **no** |

CAP-84's muxed contract addresses (`W…`, arriving with Protocol 30) do not
change this — a muxed contract address is also not a classic payment
destination.

We return the contract address rather than inventing a classic one because that
is where the money actually is. A `G…` address that merely forwarded would be a
second place for funds to go wrong.

## On-chain reference

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Registry contract | `CA7BWBV2L67YVIZWJKLYT3RRCTFCZPN6L5SXYQ4YELWP56E34JFNOEP4` |
| Network | Stellar mainnet |
| Payment asset | USDC — `CCW67TSZV3SSS2HXMBQ5JFGCKJNXKZM7UQUWUZPUTHXSTZLEO7SJMI75` |
| USDC issuer | `GA5ZSEJYB37JRC5AVCIA5MOP4RHTM335X2KGX3IHOJAPP5RE34K4KZVN` |

Read-only entry points anyone can call: `quote(name, years)`, `resolve(name)`,
`state(name)`, `expires_at(name)`, `get_admin()`.

State-changing: `register(buyer, name, years)`, `renew(payer, name, years)`,
`set_target(name, target)`.

<Callout type="warning">
Unlike the Veil wallet and factory, this contract **can be upgraded** by its
admin. That is deliberate: the registry holds names people paid for and cannot
be replaced by deploying a second one, so a price band or a window length that
turns out wrong has to be fixable. The cost is that the admin is a trusted
party, and the registry should not be described as trustless until that role
sits behind a multisig. See
[Contract Upgrades & Immutability](/contract-upgrades).
</Callout>

## Storage rent

Stellar charges rent for stored data rather than selling permanent storage.
Every entry has a time-to-live, and an entry whose TTL lapses is *archived* —
not destroyed, but unreadable until it is restored.

The registry handles this for you: **every read or write of a name pushes that
name's TTL back to 30 days**, so a name anyone actually uses keeps itself alive
as a side effect of being used. A name nobody touches for a month archives and
needs a small restore before it resolves again. The holder's ownership and
expiry date are unaffected either way.
